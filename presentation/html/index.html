Forty-seven classes, 14,774 images, mildly imbalanced — Yucca has only 66 images while Monstera Deliciosa has 547. Mean is 314 images per class, median 318. Train/val/test: 10,341 / 2,216 / 2,217 — stratified, seed 42, CSV manifests committed. We balance classes in two layers: (1) a WeightedRandomSampler at the DataLoader oversamples minority classes so each minibatch is balanced BEFORE the model sees it — measured ratio drops from 8.3x to 1.36x; (2) class-weighted cross-entropy as a safety net for any residual skew. Plus standard augmentation. All 47 species are listed in the expandable section — these are the most common indoor plants worldwide. Care knowledge base curated by hand from ASPCA toxicity database (US vet standard), RHS plant finder, and Missouri Botanical Garden. Pet safety breakdown: 34 safe, 7 mild, 53 toxic records across cats and dogs (94 total).
LeafLensThree transfer-learning baselines

Architectures compared

EfficientNet-B0

4.1 M parameters

Lightweight CNN — fastest inference, smallest footprint.

ResNet-50

23.6 M parameters

Strong classical baseline — 80.9% on ImageNet (V2 weights).

ViT-Base/16

85.8 M parameters

Modern transformer reference — sanity check on small data.

Training recipe — identical across all three

AdamW + class-weighted cross-entropy. 3-epoch frozen-backbone warm-up, then differential learning rates (1e-4 backbone / 1e-3 head). Early stopping (patience 5). 224 × 224 inputs. Batch 32 for CNNs, 16 for ViT.

Three architectures. All pretrained on ImageNet. Heads replaced with a 47-way linear layer. Same training recipe across all three for a fair comparison: AdamW, class-weighted loss, three epochs frozen then unfreeze with two different learning rates, early stopping. Batch sizes differ — ViT needs smaller because of memory.

ResNet-50 wins on top-1 and weighted F1 by a small margin. EfficientNet leads macro F1, which weights all classes equally — it's slightly better on the rarer species. ViT trails both CNN baselines. The probable reason: this dataset is in ViT's small-data weakness zone. Both course targets cleared. ResNet-50 goes into production.

Mitigations shipped

  • The UI always shows the top-3 alternatives — the user picks if the model is close.
  • Below 40% top-1 confidence, the result card is replaced by an explicit “try a better photo” banner.
  • Augmentation (rotation, flip, color jitter) makes the model robust to phone-photo variations.
Confusion matrix highlights — these are the residual hard cases. Visually similar plants share leaf shape, color, and growth habit. We mitigate by always returning top-3, and by surfacing a clear banner when confidence is low. The model is not allowed to silently guess.

Frontend on Vercel, backend on Render free tier, Postgres on Render. Inference runs in the same Flask process — the model is loaded once at boot and reused. No separate GPU node, no queue, no microservices. Right scale for the academic deliverable.

Future work

1

Bigger catalog

Expand to 100+ species by combining Kaggle with a curated PlantNet-300K subset.

2

On-device inference

Export to CoreML so prediction runs in the mobile browser — no upload, no server cost, full privacy.

Disease overlay

A second classifier trained on PlantVillage flags pests and diseases on top of the species.

4

Care timeline

Move beyond a static card to a live schedule with push reminders for watering, fertilizing, re-potting.

Catalog expansion is the obvious next step. On-device inference is the right long-term move for privacy and latency. Disease overlay is a separate classifier on top. Care timeline is where this turns into a product, not just a demo.
